04 · Personal baseline worksheet: noise vs. real change

Not medical advice. A statistics worksheet for your own records. It doesn't tell you what's healthy, and it doesn't predict anything. Printable version: 04-baseline-worksheet.html.

Daily readings like resting heart rate, HRV and sleep duration bounce around from day to day. The aim here is to learn how big your normal bounce is before you call anything a change.

Step 1 · Fix the conditions

Same device, same time (e.g. on waking), same position. HRV is especially sensitive to conditions and length of the recording (S5). Write down your conditions:

Signal: ________ Device/source: ________ When: ________ Position: ________

Step 3 · How much change is bigger than noise?

  • Typical error. Day-to-day SD within a stable period is your noise (S3 covers reliability and typical error in measurement).
  • Average before you compare. Weekly averages are steadier than single days. People monitoring HRV in sport use rolling 7-day averages for this reason (S6).
  • Rule of thumb you can write down: a weekly mean that sits outside baseline mean ± 2 × (daily SD ÷ √7) for 2+ weeks is worth a note. That's a statement about your data, not about your health.

My threshold for weekly ________: ______ ± ______

Step 4 · Two devices? Bland-Altman in plain words

For each overlap day write B − A. Bias = mean of those differences. Limits of agreement = bias ± 1.96 × SD of the differences (S2). If the limits are wide, the devices can't be swapped, no matter how well they correlate.

Step 5 · Slow drifts: CUSUM in plain words

A CUSUM adds up small deviations from your baseline so a slow, steady shift becomes visible (S4). 1. Pick k = 0.5 × SD (ignore wiggles smaller than this). 2. Each day: S⁺ = max(0, S⁺ + (x − mean − k)), S⁻ = max(0, S⁻ + (mean − x − k)). 3. If S⁺ or S⁻ goes above h = 4–5 × SD, note "sustained shift from date ___". Reset both to 0 and set a new baseline.

Step 6 · Before calling it a change, check

  • [ ] Same source the whole time? (else see 02)
  • [ ] Same measurement conditions?
  • [ ] Bigger than my threshold for 2+ weeks?
  • [ ] Any context (season, travel, illness, schedule) that explains it?

If something worries you about your health, talk to a clinician. This worksheet can't help with that.
