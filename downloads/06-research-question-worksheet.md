# 06 · Personal research question worksheet: ask something your records can answer

**Not medical advice.** A planning worksheet for questions about your own records and everyday routines. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or predict anything. Don't use it to start, stop, or change any medication or treatment; that's a conversation with a clinician. Printable version: [`06-research-question-worksheet.html`](06-research-question-worksheet.html).

Years of records invite questions like "do I sleep less on weeks with late screens?" This sheet helps you turn a hunch into a question that your data can actually answer, and write down the answer honestly, including "can't tell".

## Step 1 · Write the question in one line
Keep it about **a routine you can switch on and off** and **one signal you already record**.

`When I ________ (routine A) instead of ________ (routine B), does my ________ (signal) differ?`

Good examples: screen-off time, evening walk vs no walk, caffeine cut-off time, bedroom temperature. Avoid anything that needs a clinician to supervise.

## Step 2 · Watch for regression to the mean
Unusually high or low readings tend to be followed by readings closer to your usual level, so natural variation can look like real change ([S8](SOURCES.md)). If you only start an experiment *because* last week looked bad, the next week will often look better on its own.

- [ ] I picked the start date in advance, not right after an unusual week.
- [ ] I have a baseline from [worksheet 04](04-baseline-worksheet.md) (my normal day-to-day bounce).

## Step 3 · Plan alternating periods, decided in advance

## Step 5 · Neighbouring days are not independent
Daily readings often resemble the day before. An autocorrelation check tests that, and if the data aren't random, the familiar standard error formula s/√N isn't valid ([S11](SOURCES.md)). Practical consequence: compare **period means**, not hundreds of single days, and don't treat 28 days as 28 independent tries.

## Step 6 · Write the answer, including "can't tell"
- Difference of means, B − A: ______
- Did every pair point the same way? ☐ yes ☐ no
- Is the difference bigger than my threshold from worksheet 04? ☐ yes ☐ no
- My conclusion: ☐ looks different ☐ no visible difference ☐ can't tell (too noisy / too few periods)

This is a statement about your records under your conditions, not about health. Add the result to your [annual review](05-annual-review-worksheet.md).
